## Course Focus

This course explores how the human brain can inspire more efficient, adaptive, and intelligent AI systems. Major topics include:

- Biological vs. artificial neural networks
- Brain anatomy and computational neuroscience
- Cortical columns and distributed world models
- Spiking neural networks and neuromorphic computing
- Sensory processing and perception
- Memory, learning, attention, and consciousness
- Decision-making and autonomous systems
- Neuro-symbolic AI and cognitive architectures
- Bio-inspired AI system evaluation

## Learning Reflection

This course helped me understand that artificial intelligence is inspired by the brain, but it is not a direct copy of biological intelligence. Biological systems are adaptive, energy-efficient, distributed, event-driven, and robust under uncertainty. Artificial systems are fast, scalable, and powerful, but they often lack the flexibility and continuous learning found in biological systems.

Through the assignments and labs, I explored how neuroscience concepts such as spiking neurons, cortical columns, memory systems, perception, attention, and world models can guide the design of future AI systems. This portfolio represents my progress in connecting biological intelligence with practical AI engineering.
